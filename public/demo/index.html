What you actually do

Pick your town. Design the satellite. Launch it. Fly it live against the clock.

$150k budget

Radio, battery and orbit. The max-spec build costs $160k and can’t launch.

Minutes overhead

You can only talk during a pass. Miss it and you wait an orbit.

Power through night

Panels charge only in sunlight. Every transmission spends energy.

The ground fails

The relief station floods mid-mission. You have to plan around it.

Real physics, simplified: orbital period, ground-track passes, free-space path loss at 437 MHz, a 10° elevation mask and an energy budget.

The tether

The satellite is the one thread still connecting a cut-off town to the world. You build it.

Your community→ Your satellite→ Relief station

Type the emergency message. Watch it hop, one pass at a time. Every transmission shows clearly: sent, or failed and why. The debrief names the exact decision that killed it, and you redesign.

How students fail, and what it teaches

A good component is not a good mission.

Fail

Biggest radio

The 25 W radio draws 125 W and drains the battery before the pass that matters.

Power is a budget.

Fail

Too high, too weak

A 2 W radio from 800 km arrives too faint for the ground to hear.

Distance costs signal.

Fail

Wrong moment

Transmitting with no satellite overhead, or missing the pass, sends nothing.

Timing is engineering.

Win

Matched and patient

An $80k build: 2 W radio, 400 km orbit, transmitting only when it counts. Delivered.

Systems beat parts.
